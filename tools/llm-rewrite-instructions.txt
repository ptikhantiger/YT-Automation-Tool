The final script must never feel like it was copied, translated, AI-generated, or rewritten from another script.

# PEOPLE AND NAMES

When the story follows specific ordinary people — one, two, or three
individuals whose personal story or example the video is built around — do
NOT use their real first names or full names in the rewrite. Replace every
such name with a plain, relatable stand-in, and keep the SAME stand-in for
that same person all the way through so the audience never loses track of
who is who.

Pick the stand-in from the relationship the script implies, for example:
"a man", "a woman", "this guy", "someone", "one person", "your friend",
"a friend of yours", "your mate", "a classmate", "a colleague", "a coworker",
"your neighbor", "an old schoolmate". Always match the person's gender
("the man" / "the woman", "he" / "she"). Where no relationship is implied,
use a neutral stand-in ("a man", "a woman", "one person").

When there are two or three such people, give each a DIFFERENT fixed
stand-in so they never blur together — for example one is "your friend" and
the other is "a coworker of his", or "the first man" and "the other man".
Keep the relationships between them intact without naming anyone
("his sister", "her husband", "their manager", "an old friend of hers").

This name-swap is deliberate and required. It does NOT count as inventing a
person or changing a fact: the person, everything they do, and every real
detail stay exactly the same — only the name is removed.

EXCEPTION: genuinely well-known public figures who are central to a factual
account — heads of state, government officials, world-famous business or
historical figures — keep their real names, because a generic stand-in
would make the account inaccurate or absurd. Everyone else is anonymized as
above.

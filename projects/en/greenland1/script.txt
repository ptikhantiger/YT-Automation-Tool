Picture a place where you could nuke the largest city in Greenland. Located far in the northern hemisphere with most of its land frozen, this place presents an interesting story. It's a remote zone with a vast expanse of ice that still exudes an undeniable natural charm. Behind the silence and the cold, there's a real pulse of life. This is Nuuk, the largest city in Greenland, isolated by eternal ice with a very long winter every year. 

Greenland is the largest island in the world. It sits between the Arctic Ocean and the Atlantic Ocean, northeast of Canada. Most of its territory is covered in ice all year round, but this place is inhabited by 56,000 residents. Of all the inhabited places, Nuuk is the largest city, and it accommodates a third of Greenland's population. Nuuk is really beautiful and colorful in the summer. However, when winter rolls in, the face of the city instantly transforms into a white world covered in frozen snow. 

According to history, it was founded in 1728 by the Norwegian missionary Hans Egede and originally named Godthab, which means Good Hope. Before Europeans arrived, the area around Nuuk had been inhabited by various Inuit groups for thousands of years. These Inuit people were hunters and fishermen who relied heavily on natural resources to survive. 

Along the way, Nuuk continued to grow and develop. The city plays an important role in Greenland's history, especially after Greenland gained autonomy from Denmark in 1979. Nuuk serves as the administrative center for the local government of Greenland, making it the capital of the northernmost country in the world. 

Right now, Nuuk is inhabited by around 19,872 residents. They live in houses made with bright, vibrant colors. Since colonial times, greenlandic houses have tended to be small and brightly painted. Historically, each color had a specific meaning that showed what the building was used for. A red building symbolized a church or school. A yellow building meant the occupant was a doctor. A green house indicated that the person living there was a mechanic or worked in the electrical or telecommunications industry. A blue house was for employees in the fishing industry, while a black building was a police station. 

Until now, colorful houses remain a trademark of Greenland. Only now, residents can choose whatever color they want without having to match their profession. 

Extreme weather is one of the toughest challenges in this city, which is famous for its polar climate and long winter. For about seven months every year, the temperature here drops below freezing. March is usually the coldest month, which leaves the entire city wrapped in thick ice. Nuuk goes through a very short summer that lasts only three months. Even though it's summer, the temperature still feels cold, averaging around nine degrees Celsius. On December 21st, Nuuk experiences the shortest day and longest night of the year. On that day, the sun doesn't even peek out until 11:22 in the morning.

and sets at 3:28 p.m. On the flip side, on June 21st, Nuuk gets the longest day and shortest night of the year. The sun rises at 3:53 a.m. and only sets at 1:00 a.m., meaning you can actually see the sun shining at night. As a remote city surrounded by frozen ice, it's already got pretty much everything you'd need. You can find airports, hospitals, shopping centers, schools, and universities. In fact, the only university in the whole Greenland region is right here in Nuuk. Electricity is a big deal for any city, and this place is well sorted with its own power systems. Nuuk gets its juice from hydroelectric power plants, with the main one being the Buksefjord hydroelectric plant, sitting about 45 kilometers outside the city. It harnesses the natural flow of water coming off the glaciers to generate electricity. On top of hydro power, they're also pushing for more wind energy to tap into other renewables. Getting to Nuuk isn't easy since it's a remote city with zero roads leading into it. Planes and ships are your only two options for travel. But in the winter, ships just aren't reliable because the surrounding waters freeze right up. If you really have to go by water, you'll need a special icebreaker to carve a path through for other boats. Even though it's isolated and deals with long, harsh winters, Nuuk is blessed with some jaw-dropping natural scenery. Just like in other polar regions, the night sky often lights up with colorful auroras dancing across the horizon, turning the city nights into something truly magical. At the end of the day, Nuuk is the northernmost capital on Earth and the most populated settlement on the Greenland mainland. Sure, most of Greenland is covered in thick ice with extreme weather that lasts forever, but life here keeps thriving. It's backed by solid infrastructure, including a big port and an international airport that keep the economy moving through services, fishing, and tourism. With all that spectacular natural beauty like giant icebergs and deep blue seas, Nuuk has become an incredible spot for anyone looking for a real Arctic adventure.
